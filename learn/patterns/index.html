How to read the stats (2 minutes)

Most pattern numbers come from Thomas Bulkowski (Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns, thepatternsite.com). His sample is US stocks in bull markets. They are the best public pattern stats there are, and they are not crypto.

Break even failure rate = share of patterns that fail to move at least 5% in the breakout direction, roughly the cost of trading. Lower is better.

Average rise is measured from breakout to the "ultimate high," the top before a 20% drop. Nobody sells the exact top, so your real result is smaller. Treat it as a ranking tool, not a promise.

Target rule: Bulkowski scales the pattern height by the share of patterns that reached the full target. That is why targets below say "height times 61%" and the like.

Indicator numbers come from academic papers and named backtests. Where none exist, the card says no solid stats found and gives a straight verdict anyway.

The big picture from the research: technical rules showed real profits in crypto in sample (Hudson & Urquhart, nearly 15,000 rules), but no out-of-sample predictability for Bitcoin itself. In big US stocks, moving average edges largely vanish after realistic costs (Bajgrowicz & Scaillet). The edge that survives best is trend following and levels, not reversal oscillators or candles.

Cliff-notes cards

Cup and handle Lead example
Real edgeChart pattern
Rank 3 of 39 bullish patterns. 5% break even failure. 54% average rise. 61% hit target. 913 trades.

What it is. A rounded U-shaped base, then a small drift down (the handle) in the upper half of the cup, then a breakout over the rim. Bulkowski wants the cup 7 to 65 weeks long. Weekly charts show it best.

EntryClose above the right rim. Early entry: close above a down-sloping trendline drawn across the handle highs.
StopUnder the handle low. Raise it as price climbs.
TargetCup depth (rim to bottom) times 61%, added to the breakout price.

When it helps. U shape, not V. Handle shorter than the 22-day median does better (Bulkowski). Breakout on rising volume. Works best when the whole market is in an uptrend.

When it fails. Bulkowski’s own 300-pattern lesson: 47% of cups retraced significantly within two months of breakout, and 23% rose no more than 15% before dropping. Throwbacks (62% of the time) hurt performance. In crypto, a V-shaped cup on a 1-hour chart is not this pattern.

Crypto 24/7 note. Use daily or weekly. 24/7 trading means the handle often gets a weekend wick below it; judge on closes, not wicks.

Source: Bulkowski, thepatternsite.com: Cup with Handle Bulkowski: Glossary (break even failure rate, ultimate high)

Head and shoulders top
Real edgeChart pattern
Rank 9 of 36 bearish patterns. 19% failure. 16% average decline. 51% hit target. 2,800+ trades. Also profitable in FX (Osler & Chang) and statistically informative on NYSE/AMEX (Lo et al.).

What it is. Three peaks, the middle one highest, after an uptrend. The neckline joins the two armpits (the lows between peaks).

EntryClose below the neckline (or below the right armpit if the neckline slopes down). Short, or exit longs.
StopAbove the right shoulder.
TargetHead-to-neckline height times 51%, subtracted from the breakout.

When it helps. Proportional, symmetric shoulders. Volume highest on the left shoulder and falling into the right. Osler & Chang (1995) found an objective, computerized version earned significant profits across six currencies combined, 1973 to 1994, robust to costs and risk.

When it fails. Pullback to the neckline happens 68% of the time, so a tight stop under the neckline gets hit. Osler & Chang found it worked for the mark and yen but not for the pound, Swiss franc, French franc or Canadian dollar. Lopsided patterns are noise.

Crypto 24/7 note. Shorting crypto is where most people get hurt. In a bull year, use the top as an exit or trim signal on spot, not a leveraged short.

Source: Bulkowski: Head-and-Shoulders Top Osler & Chang (1995), Head and Shoulders: Not Just a Flaky Pattern, NY Fed Staff Report 4 Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000), Foundations of Technical Analysis, Journal of Finance

Inverse head and shoulders
Real edgeChart pattern
Rank 13 of 39. 11% failure. 45% average rise. 71% hit target. 3,197 trades. Lo et al. found the inverse version NOT significant on NYSE/AMEX.

What it is. Mirror of the top: three valleys, the middle lowest, after a downtrend.

EntryClose above the neckline (or above the right armpit if the neckline slopes up).
StopUnder the right shoulder low.
TargetHead-to-neckline height times 71%, added to the breakout.

When it helps. Volume highest on the left shoulder or head, fading on the right. Shoulders bottom near the same price. Lives at a big zoomed-out level.

When it fails. 65% throw back to the neckline. Academic support is weaker than for the top version.

Crypto 24/7 note. Very common on BTC 4-hour charts. Only count it on daily or higher if you want the Bulkowski numbers to mean anything.

Source: Bulkowski: Head-and-Shoulders Bottom Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000), Foundations of Technical Analysis, Journal of Finance

Double top
Context-onlyChart pattern
Confirmed Eve & Eve tops: rank 12 of 36, 20% failure, 16% average decline. Unconfirmed twin peaks fail 60% of the time.

What it is. Two peaks near the same price after an uptrend, with a valley between.

EntryOnly after a close below the valley between the peaks. Before that it is just two highs.
StopAbove the higher peak.
TargetPeak-to-valley height times 43% (Eve & Eve) to 64% (Adam & Adam), subtracted from the valley.

When it helps. Wait for confirmation. Bulkowski: unconfirmed twin peaks fail 60%; confirmed Adam & Adam tops fail 25%.

When it fails. Calling a double top before the valley breaks is the classic mistake; price usually just breaks out higher.

Crypto 24/7 note. Equal highs in crypto are a stop and liquidation magnet. Expect a sweep above the second peak before any real drop.

Source: Bulkowski: Eve & Eve Double Top Bulkowski: Adam & Adam Double Top

Double bottom
Real edgeChart pattern
Eve & Eve: rank 5 of 39, 12% failure, 50% average rise, 952 trades. Adam & Adam: 16% failure, 39% rise.

What it is. Two lows near the same price after a decline, with a peak between.

EntryClose above the peak between the two lows.
StopUnder the lower of the two lows.
TargetHeight times 65% (Eve & Eve), added to the confirmation price.

When it helps. Wide, rounded bottoms (Eve) beat narrow spiky ones (Adam). Usually 2 to 7 weeks apart.

When it fails. Throwbacks 65% of the time. Buying the second low before confirmation is gambling.

Crypto 24/7 note. The second low often undercuts the first by a wick (a spring). That is fine; judge the close.

Source: Bulkowski: Eve & Eve Double Bottom Bulkowski: Adam & Adam Double Bottom

Triple top
Context-onlyChart pattern
Rank 24 of 36. 25% failure. 14% average decline. 49% hit target. 1,964 trades.

What it is. Three peaks near the same price, breakout down through the lowest valley.

EntryClose below the lowest valley.
StopAbove the highest peak.
TargetHeight times 49%, subtracted from the lowest valley.

When it helps. More common in bear markets. Fine as a reason to take profit.

When it fails. Middle of the pack. Three tests of resistance more often means resistance is getting absorbed.

Crypto 24/7 note. Treat repeated equal highs as fuel for a squeeze until the low breaks.

Source: Bulkowski: Triple Top

Triple bottom
Real edgeChart pattern
Rank 12 of 39. 13% failure. 46% average rise. 74% hit target. 2,500+ trades.

What it is. Three lows near the same price, breakout up over the highest peak between them.

EntryClose above the highest peak in the pattern.
StopUnder the lowest low.
TargetHeight times 74%, added to the breakout.

When it helps. A level defended three times is exactly the kind of zoomed-out level your first pillar looks for.

When it fails. Do not buy the third touch blind; it breaks often enough to hurt.

Crypto 24/7 note. Watch for the third low to wick under the first two. A reclaim close is the tell.

Source: Bulkowski: Triple Bottom

Ascending triangle
Context-onlyChart pattern
Up breakouts: rank 16 of 39, 17% failure, 43% rise. Down breakouts: 38% failure, 13% decline. 1,400+ trades.

What it is. Flat top, rising lows. Buyers keep stepping up into a fixed ceiling.

EntryClose above the flat top.
StopUnder the last rising low.
TargetTriangle height times 70%, added to the breakout.

When it helps. Trade the upward breakout only. Volume falls through the pattern (it does in 78%+ of cases).

When it fails. If it breaks down instead, it is a weak short (38% failure). Do not pre-buy on the assumption it must break up.

Crypto 24/7 note. The flat top is a wall of short stops. Fake pops above it are common on perps; demand a candle close plus a retest.

Source: Bulkowski: Ascending Triangle

Descending triangle
Context-onlyChart pattern
Breaks UP 53% of the time. Down breakouts: rank 15 of 36, 23% failure, 15% decline. Up breakouts: rank 33 of 39.

What it is. Flat floor, falling highs.

EntryWait for the close outside either line. Short a close below the floor or buy a close above the top line.
StopOpposite side of the triangle.
TargetHeight times 50% (down) or 64% (up).

When it helps. Down breakouts are a decent short in a bear market.

When it fails. The lore says it is bearish. The data says it breaks up slightly more often than down. Never pre-position.

Crypto 24/7 note. Flat floors attract long liquidations below them. Breakdowns can overshoot fast and then snap back.

Source: Bulkowski: Descending Triangle

Symmetrical triangle
Mostly old wives’ taleChart pattern
Rank 36 of 39 (up) and 34 of 36 (down). 25%/37% failure. Breaks up 60% of the time. 3,000+ trades.

What it is. Lower highs and higher lows converging toward an apex.

EntryClose outside a trendline. Breakouts usually come about 74% of the way to the apex.
StopBack inside the triangle.
TargetHeight times 58% (up) or 36% (down).

When it helps. Useful only as a volatility-squeeze map: something is about to move.

When it fails. Near the bottom of Bulkowski’s performance table in both directions. It says almost nothing about direction.

Crypto 24/7 note. Classic fakeout zone on BTC. The first break is often the wrong one.

Source: Bulkowski: Symmetrical Triangle

Bull and bear flags
Context-onlyChart pattern
44%/45% failure (up/down). Average move just 9%/8%. 46% hit target. Short-term swing measured.

What it is. A steep pole, then a short tilted rectangle against the trend, under 3 weeks.

EntryClose above the flag top (bull) or below the flag bottom (bear).
StopOther side of the flag.
TargetPole length added at the flag (half-staff idea), but expect less: 46% reach it.

When it helps. Only in a strong, steep trend with falling volume in the flag (74% to 77% of the time it falls).

When it fails. Near coin-flip failure rates. Ordinary flags are not the edge Twitter thinks they are.

Crypto 24/7 note. Bear flags in crypto downtrends work better as a reason not to buy than as a short.

Source: Bulkowski: Flags

High and tight flag
Real edgeChart pattern
15% failure. 39% average rise. 82% hit a half-height target. 1,028 trades.

What it is. Price at least doubles (90%+) in 2 months or less, then pauses tightly.

EntryClose above the highest peak in the pattern (usually the pole top).
StopUnder the pause low.
TargetHalf the pole height, added to the breakout.

When it helps. This is the flag that actually works. Receding volume in the pause.

When it fails. Rare by definition, and the stop can be wide.

Crypto 24/7 note. Common in small caps and new listings. Liquidity can vanish; size small.

Source: Bulkowski: High and Tight Flag

Pennants
Mostly old wives’ taleChart pattern
54% failure both ways. Average move 7%/6%. 35%/32% hit target. 1,600+ trades.

What it is. A pole, then a tiny symmetrical triangle under 3 weeks.

EntryClose outside the pennant in the pole direction.
StopOpposite side.
TargetDo not count on the pole projection.

When it helps. Nothing reliable. Treat as a pause, not a signal.

When it fails. Fails more often than it works by Bulkowski’s measure.

Crypto 24/7 note. Same verdict. Lower timeframe pennants are noise.

Source: Bulkowski: Pennants

Rising wedge
Mostly old wives’ taleChart pattern
Breaks down 60% of the time, but the down breakout ranks 36 of 36 (dead last): 51% failure, 9% average decline.

What it is. Two rising, converging lines. Lore says bearish.

EntryIf you trade it at all: close below the lower line.
StopAbove the last high.
TargetSmall. Expect single digits.

When it helps. As a warning that a rally is tiring. Fine for trimming.

When it fails. As a short it is the worst bearish pattern on the list. Direction right, payoff wrong.

Crypto 24/7 note. Rising wedges on alts often end with a squeeze up first. Do not short them with leverage.

Source: Bulkowski: Rising Wedge

Falling wedge
Context-onlyChart pattern
Breaks up 68% of the time. Up breakout: rank 31 of 39, 26% failure, 38% rise. 800+ trades.

What it is. Two falling, converging lines.

EntryClose above the upper line.
StopUnder the wedge low.
TargetBack to the wedge’s highest peak first.

When it helps. Direction bias is real (68% up). Best at a zoomed-out support level.

When it fails. Middling payoff and a 26% failure rate. Pullback after a down break is 74%.

Crypto 24/7 note. Decent pairing with a spring at the wedge low.

Source: Bulkowski: Falling Wedge

Rectangles and ranges
Real edgeChart pattern
Rectangle tops, up breakout: rank 4 of 39, 15% failure, 51% rise. Bottoms, up breakout: rank 8 of 39, 48% rise. Bitcoin: trading range breakout was the only rule that consistently beat buy-and-hold Sharpe, 2011 to 2019.

What it is. Price ping-pongs between a flat ceiling and flat floor, then leaves.

EntryClose outside the range. Upward breakouts are the money side.
StopBack inside, under the range mid or floor.
TargetRange height times 78% to 79% (up), added to the breakout.

When it helps. Long ranges on daily or weekly. Gerritsen et al. found 150 and 200-day breakout rules did best in trending years (2013 to 2014, 2017 to 2018).

When it fails. Down breakouts are weaker (34% failure from tops). In quiet years (2015 to 2016) the BTC breakout rule lagged buy-and-hold.

Crypto 24/7 note. This is the most crypto-tested pattern on the list. Range highs and lows are where the liquidations sit.

Source: Bulkowski: Rectangle Tops Bulkowski: Rectangle Bottoms Gerritsen, Bouri, Ramezanifar & Roubaud (2020), Profitability of technical trading rules in the Bitcoin market, Finance Research Letters

Rounding bottom
Real edgeChart pattern
Rank 7 of 39. 4% failure (lowest on this list). 48% average rise. 990 trades.

What it is. A slow, bowl-shaped turn, usually over many months, easiest to see on weekly.

EntryClose above the left lip.
StopUnder the bowl low or a later higher low.
TargetBowl depth, added to the lip, scaled by the 65% hit rate.

When it helps. Weekly chart, months long. Often acts as a continuation after a prior uptrend (67%).

When it fails. Slow. Entering mid-bowl ties up capital for months.

Crypto 24/7 note. Fits post-bear accumulation in BTC on the weekly. Rare and useful.

Source: Bulkowski: Rounding Bottom

Gaps (and "all gaps fill")
Context-onlyChart pattern
Upward gaps closing within a week: area 85%, exhaustion 60%, continuation 8%, breakaway 1%. 1,100+ samples.

What it is. A price jump that leaves an empty space on the chart.

EntryNo standalone entry. Hold a winner that breaks out of a pattern on a gap.
StopBelow the gap for a breakaway gap.
TargetNone from the gap itself.

When it helps. Bulkowski: when a chart pattern breaks out on a gap, performance improved two-thirds of the time.

When it fails. "All gaps fill" is false for breakaway gaps: 1% close within a week. By the time you classify a gap, the move is mostly done.

Crypto 24/7 note. Crypto does not gap 24/7. The famous "CME gap" is from weekend futures closures; no solid stats found on CME gap fills.

Source: Bulkowski: Gaps

Wyckoff accumulation and distribution (spring, upthrust)
Context-onlyFrameworkno solid stats
No academic stats found. One public mechanical crypto test of the spring: 2,058 trades, 33% win rate, minus 0.21% per trade, profit factor 0.59 (blog, not peer reviewed).

What it is. A range where big players absorb supply (accumulation) or distribute it. The spring is a fake break under the range that snaps back; the upthrust is the mirror above.

EntryNot on the spring alone. Buy the reclaim close back inside the range only when the range sits on a zoomed-out level and macro is not hostile.
StopUnder the spring wick.
TargetRange top first, then the measured move.

When it helps. As confirmation stacked on a level people defend. That is how your pillar three uses it, and that is the only way it earns its keep.

When it fails. As a mechanical trigger it lost money in the only crypto backtest found: a spring looks identical to a range starting to fail. The story is unfalsifiable.

Crypto 24/7 note. Every crypto range low gets swept. That is why the sweep alone carries no information.

Source: Hedonist Research, mechanical crypto backtest of the Wyckoff spring (blog, not peer reviewed)

Elliott Wave
Mostly old wives’ taleFrameworkno solid stats
No solid stats found showing an edge. A forex thesis found no statistically significant predictive power.

What it is. A theory that markets move in 5 waves with the trend and 3 against it.

EntryNone that two analysts would agree on in real time.
StopWave rules, which get relabeled after the fact.
TargetFibonacci projections.

When it helps. As a vocabulary for "this trend is extended." That is it.

When it fails. Counts are revised after price moves, so it can never be wrong. That is the definition of untestable.

Crypto 24/7 note. Crypto Twitter’s favorite. Ignore the count, keep the levels it happens to share with real structure.

Source: Nazarbayev University thesis, Elliott Wave on forex time series

Fibonacci retracements and extensions
Mostly old wives’ taleFramework
No solid peer-reviewed stats. One unrefereed 2022 preprint claims 38.2/50/61.8% levels predict index and FX returns.

What it is. Draw from swing low to swing high; watch 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% for pullback support and 1.272/1.618 for targets.

EntryOnly where a fib level lines up with real prior structure (an old high, a range edge).
StopBelow the next level or the structure.
TargetPrior high, then 1.272 or 1.618 extension as a profit-taking zone.

When it helps. Self-fulfilling where enough people stare at it, and as a neutral way to plan scaling out.

When it fails. With five levels between any high and low, price always lands near one. That is not prediction.

Crypto 24/7 note. Popular enough on BTC that 0.618 sometimes acts like a round number. Treat it as a crowd level, not math.

Source: Shanaev & Gibson (2022), Can Returns Breed Like Rabbits? Econometric Tests for Fibonacci Retracements (SSRN preprint)

Support and resistance, round numbers
Real edgeLevel
Osler (2000): published S/R levels predicted intraday FX reversals; over 70% of levels ended in 0 and 96% in 0 or 5. Bitcoin daily: range breakouts beat buy-and-hold Sharpe 2011 to 2019 (Gerritsen et al.).

What it is. Prices where orders cluster: prior highs and lows, range edges, round numbers. Osler later showed why: take-profit orders cluster at round numbers and stop orders just beyond them.

EntryBuy reactions at defended levels or breaks that hold. Your pillar one: zoomed-out levels people defend or run from.
StopJust beyond the level plus room for the stop run.
TargetNext major level.

When it helps. Higher timeframe, obvious, round-number levels. This has the best mechanism and the best academic support of anything on the chart.

When it fails. Stops sit just beyond obvious levels, so price pokes through them first. Bulkowski: a candle window (gap) supports price only 20% of the time.

Crypto 24/7 note. Round numbers (100k BTC, 4k ETH) are real magnets. Expect the wick through them.

Source: Osler (2000), Support for Resistance, NY Fed Economic Policy Review Bulkowski: Support and Resistance Gerritsen, Bouri, Ramezanifar & Roubaud (2020), Profitability of technical trading rules in the Bitcoin market, Finance Research Letters

SR flip / BCB retest (break, close, back-test)
Real edgeLevel
58% of 10,305 upward breakouts threw back. Those that held at or above the breakout rose 40% on average vs 29% for those that dipped below. 65% recover after a throwback.

What it is. Price breaks a level, closes beyond it, comes back to test it from the other side, and holds.

EntryOn the hold of the retest (a rejection candle at the old level).
StopA close back through the level.
TargetNext level, or the pattern’s measured move.

When it helps. High-volume breakouts throw back 70% of the time, so patience pays. Buying the retest gives a tighter stop than buying the breakout.

When it fails. If the retest closes back through, 35% continue below the pattern. Get out on the close, do not argue.

Crypto 24/7 note. Retests in crypto overshoot with wicks. Use closes on the timeframe you entered on.

Source: Bulkowski: Throwbacks Bulkowski: Pullbacks

Trendlines
Context-onlyLevel
Trading up-trendline breaks: 3,172 trades, average gain 0.7%, median loss 0.9%, 37% win/loss. Long, shallow lines with 4+ touches did better.

What it is. A line connecting rising lows or falling highs.

EntryA close through a long, shallow, many-touch line, ideally on a throwback.
StopBack across the line.
TargetHeight times 56% to 63% (Bulkowski measure for down/up lines).

When it helps. Long lines (over 48 days) with widely spaced touches. Shallow slopes beat steep ones.

When it fails. Short, steep, three-touch lines lost money. Trendlines are the most subjective drawing on the chart.

Crypto 24/7 note. Anyone can draw a line that fits. Prefer horizontal levels.

Source: Bulkowski: Up Trendlines Bulkowski: Down Trendlines

Engulfing candles
Mostly old wives’ taleCandlestick
Bullish engulfing: reverses 63% but ranks 84 of 103 candles. Bearish engulfing: reverses 79% but ranks 91 of 103. Candlestick rules showed no value on DJIA stocks (Marshall et al.).

What it is. A candle whose body swallows the prior opposite-colored body.

EntryOnly as a trigger at a level you already wanted to trade.
StopBeyond the engulfing candle extreme.
TargetNext level.

When it helps. As timing inside a setup that already has a reason.

When it fails. The follow-through is poor. Bulkowski: after an upward breakout of a bullish engulfing, the best 10-day move was a drop of 1.18%.

Crypto 24/7 note. Crypto has no real open and close; the daily candle depends on the exchange’s UTC cutoff. Weakens candle signals further.

Source: Bulkowski: Bullish Engulfing Bulkowski: Bearish Engulfing Marshall, Young & Rose (2006), Candlestick technical trading strategies, Journal of Banking & Finance

RSI (overbought, oversold, and the 50 line)
Context-onlyIndicator
Crypto, 2018 to 2022, daily: buying RSI under 30 returned 177.7% vs 275.2% buy-and-hold. Holding only while RSI was above 50 returned 773.7%. Shorting overbought went to zero.

What it is. A 0 to 100 momentum gauge. Textbook: above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold.

EntryUse it as a trend filter: longs only while daily RSI holds above 50 (Cardwell style).
StopYour price stop, not the RSI.
TargetExit or trim when RSI loses 50 on the daily.

When it helps. As a trend filter in crypto it beat buy-and-hold in that study. Chong & Ng (2008) found RSI rules beat buy-and-hold on 60 years of the FT30.

When it fails. As a reversal tool in crypto it is wrong. Overbought in a bull market is a sign of strength, not a top. Shorting RSI 70 bankrupted every coin in the test.

Crypto 24/7 note. Study used 0.1% fees, no slippage, and tuned rules on the same period. Treat the size of the win with caution, the direction of the finding with confidence.

Source: Zatwarnicki, Zatwarnicki & Stolarski (2023), Effectiveness of RSI Signals in Timing the Cryptocurrency Market, Sensors Chong & Ng (2008), Testing the MACD and RSI rules using the FT30, Applied Economics Letters

RSI divergence
Mostly old wives’ taleIndicator
Crypto, 2018 to 2022: buying bullish divergences on BTC lost 31.9% vs plus 243.8% buy-and-hold. Portfolio: 86.2% vs 275.2%. Authors: "proven ineffective."

What it is. Price makes a lower low while RSI makes a higher low (bullish), or the mirror (bearish).

EntryDo not trade it alone.
Stopn/a
Targetn/a

When it helps. As a "momentum is fading, tighten stops" note on a position you already hold.

When it fails. On BTC, bearish divergences were followed by bigger gains (29.99% at 60 days) than bullish ones (2.78%). The signal ran backwards.

Crypto 24/7 note. The most oversold idea in crypto education. Fade it.

MACD
Context-onlyIndicator
Crypto 2018 to 2022, long-only: MACD portfolio 539.7% vs 275.2% buy-and-hold; beat hold on 9 of 10 coins. FT30 60 years: beat buy-and-hold in most cases (Chong & Ng).

What it is. Difference of 12 and 26 EMAs with a 9 signal line. A trend-following tool dressed as an oscillator.

EntryLong when MACD crosses above signal while price is above the 200-day. Ignore crosses against the trend.
StopPrice stop under the last swing low.
TargetRide until the opposite cross on the daily, or trim at levels.

When it helps. Trending markets. It is a moving average system, so it shares the moving average edge.

When it fails. Chops you up in ranges. Hudson & Urquhart found strong in-sample crypto results but no out-of-sample predictability for Bitcoin.

Crypto 24/7 note. Use daily or higher. On 15-minute charts it is noise plus fees.

Source: Zatwarnicki, Zatwarnicki & Stolarski (2023), Effectiveness of RSI Signals in Timing the Cryptocurrency Market, Sensors Chong & Ng (2008), Testing the MACD and RSI rules using the FT30, Applied Economics Letters Hudson & Urquhart (2019/2021), Technical trading and cryptocurrencies, Annals of Operations Research

Moving averages as a trend filter (price vs 50/200)
Real edgeIndicator
Bitcoin: price-to-moving-average ratios predicted daily returns in and out of sample; Sharpe gains about 0.2 to 0.6 over buy-and-hold (Detzel et al. 2021). DJIA 1897 to 1986: buy-sell spreads significant (Brock et al. 1992).

What it is. Be long when price is above a long moving average (200-day, or the 50 for faster), flat or defensive below.

EntryDaily close above the 200-day (or reclaim and hold).
StopDaily close back below, or below the 50 for a tighter version.
TargetNone. It is a regime switch, you ride it.

When it helps. The best-supported indicator for crypto specifically. Corbet et al. found moving average rules significant on minute Bitcoin data; Detzel et al. out of sample.

When it fails. In big US stocks the edge has faded: Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012) found low transaction costs erased it. Whipsaws in sideways years.

Crypto 24/7 note. This is your macro check, pillar two, in one line: is BTC above its 200-day?

Source: Detzel, Liu, Strauss, Zhou & Zhu (2021), Learning and predictability via technical analysis: bitcoin, Financial Management Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992), Simple Technical Trading Rules, Journal of Finance Corbet, Eraslan, Lucey & Sensoy (2019), Technical trading rules in cryptocurrency markets, Finance Research Letters Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012), Technical trading revisited, Journal of Financial Economics

Golden cross and death cross (50/200)
Context-onlyIndicator
S&P 500 since 1950: 36 golden crosses, 12 months later +10.5% average, 77.8% positive. Baseline any year: +9.1%, 72.6% positive. Crosses with a falling 200-day: +16.8%, 93.3% positive.

What it is. The 50-day moving average crosses above (golden) or below (death) the 200-day.

EntryNot a trigger. The price-vs-200 filter fires earlier.
Stopn/a
Targetn/a

When it helps. The only interesting slice: a golden cross while the 200-day is still falling, which marks a turn after a bear market.

When it fails. The plain golden cross barely beats a random year (10.5% vs 9.1%). It is late by construction. No solid crypto-specific stats found for the death cross.

Bollinger Bands
Mostly old wives’ taleIndicator

What it is. A 20-period average with bands 2 standard deviations away.

EntryNot as "touch the band, fade it."
Stopn/a
Targetn/a

Volume and OBV
Context-onlyIndicator
OBV: mixed. Profitable after costs in Thailand but not Singapore (SpringerPlus 2015); profitable in Greater China (Economics Bulletin 2009). Bulkowski: high-volume breakouts throw back 70% of the time.

What it is. Volume confirms who is in control. OBV is a running total of up-day minus down-day volume.

EntryUse volume to confirm a breakout or a spring, not as an entry signal.
Stopn/a
Targetn/a

When it helps. Falling volume inside a pattern and rising volume on the break is in nearly every Bulkowski pattern profile.

When it fails. OBV as a standalone system: works in some markets, not others.

Crypto 24/7 note. Crypto volume is split across exchanges and partly fake on some venues. Use Coinbase or aggregate spot volume, not one offshore exchange.

VWAP
Context-onlyIndicator
Zarattini & Aziz (2023): long above session VWAP, short below, on QQQ 2018 to 2023 reported 671% vs 126% buy-and-hold, with a 9% max drawdown vs 36%. Independent retest (long side): Sept 2023 to Sept 2026 returned 9.7% vs 105.9% buy-and-hold, and lost money at 0.02% cost per side.

What it is. Volume-weighted average price since the session open. Institutions benchmark fills against it.

EntryIntraday only: long above, short below, flat at the close.
StopBack through VWAP.
TargetEnd of session.

When it helps. Day trading liquid stocks and ETFs where there is a real session open.

When it fails. The edge did not survive later years or realistic costs in the independent retest. About 4 round trips a day means fees eat it.

Crypto 24/7 note. No session open in crypto. Anchored VWAP from a major low or event is the crypto version; no solid stats found for it.

ATR for stops and sizing
Real edgeRisk toolno solid stats
No solid stats found for a specific ATR multiple. Kaminski & Lo (2014): stop-loss rules add value when returns have momentum, and cost you when prices are a random walk.

What it is. Average True Range: the average daily range including gaps. Measures how much a market normally wiggles.

EntrySet the stop at structure plus 1 to 2 ATR, then size the position so that stop equals your fixed risk.
StopStructure plus ATR buffer.
TargetAt least 2 times the stop distance before you take the trade.

When it helps. Crypto has momentum, which is exactly where Kaminski & Lo show stops help. ATR keeps your stop outside normal noise.

When it fails. The edge is in sizing, not prediction. The multiple you pick has no proven magic number.

Crypto 24/7 note. BTC daily ATR is several times a stock index’s. Size smaller, not tighter.

Stacking confluence: your four pillars

No single pattern or indicator above is a system. The research points one way: levels plus trend plus confirmation plus a hard exit. That is exactly your four pillars. A trade needs all four.

  1. Zoomed-out levels people defend or run from. Weekly and daily highs, lows, range edges, round numbers. This is the best-supported idea in technical analysis (Osler on order clustering; Gerritsen on Bitcoin range breakouts). If you cannot name the level, there is no trade.
  2. Macro check. Is BTC above its 200-day? Is BTC dominance rising (risk-off inside crypto)? Is funding sane? For the Schwab IRA: is the S&P above its 200-day? Trend filters are the indicator family with the best crypto evidence (Detzel et al.; Corbet et al.).
  3. Confirmation. A Wyckoff spring and reclaim, a BCB retest that holds, a high-volume close through the level. Confirmation alone loses (the spring test: 33% win rate). Confirmation at a pillar-one level, in a pillar-two trend, is the point.
  4. Invalidation plus taking profits. Stop at structure plus an ATR buffer, sized so the stop is a fixed dollar risk. Take partial profit at the next level; trail the rest. Kaminski & Lo: stops pay in momentum markets, and crypto is one.

Worked example (cup and handle on BTC weekly): cup rim sits at a round number (pillar 1). BTC above a rising 200-day, dominance flat, funding normal (pillar 2). Breakout close above the rim, then a retest that holds the rim (pillar 3). Stop under the handle low; first target at cup depth times 61%; trail the rest under weekly higher lows (pillar 4). Entry through a Coinbase limit order at the retest, never a market chase.

Scoring rule: 4 of 4 pillars, full planned size. 3 of 4, half size. 2 or fewer, no trade.
