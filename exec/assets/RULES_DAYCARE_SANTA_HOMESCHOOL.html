In-home day care

The first question is not “can I.” It is “what am I actually doing.”

Start here: is this paid, regular care (a fee, on a schedule), with the parent leaving the child with you? Florida defines child care that way in FS 402.302(1).
▼ THEN ASK WHO IS IN CARE
Green · usually not day care

If no Parents stay for a farm day, a class, or goat yoga. Or it is unpaid, occasional, and not on a regular schedule. That is an activity, not a day-care home.

Gold · one other family

If yes, one family Paid, regular care for children from only one unrelated family (plus your own) is not a “family child care home” under FS 402.302(8). The line is real. It disappears the day a second family is added. Still get a liability endorsement.

Red · two or more families

If yes, two+ families Paid, regular care in your occupied home for children from at least two unrelated families is a family child care home. In Nassau, Duval, and St. Johns, a LICENSE is required. DCF registration is not allowed in these counties.

▼ TWO MORE CHECKS, EITHER WAY
A further tripwire

More than five children unrelated to you, anywhere you operate, is a child care facility under FS 402.302(2), unless a listed exemption fits. That is a different, heavier license than a family home.

Who licenses us here

Nassau, Duval, and St. Johns require licensure of family homes, but DCF is still the licensing agency (only Broward, Palm Beach, Pinellas, and Sarasota run their own). Apply in CARES, pay the fee, pass a pre-license inspection. Unlicensed operation is a first-degree misdemeanor: FS 402.319.

Those caps are FS 402.302(8), and they include household children under 13. A “large family child care home” (up to 12, with two staff) is a later license: two years as a licensed family home, a CDA or equivalent for one year, and a 40-hour group-care course — FS 402.302(11) and 402.3131. Zoning: a licensed family home is a valid residential use. Counties and cities may not require a special permit or a zoning fee over $50 (FS 125.0109 and 166.0445). Nassau’s land-development code already lists licensed family homes as a permitted use in every residential district. HOA covenants are not preempted. If the homestead sits inside Town of Callahan limits, the town’s home-occupation rules may also appear; the state residential-use protection still applies to a licensed family home.

In-home day care

If it is a family child care home, here is the real bar.

Strict · required

  • License, not registration, in Nassau, Duval, and St. Johns. DCF issues it.
  • 30-hour intro course and exam, plus a one-time 5-hour early-literacy course, before caring for children (35 hours total). The 40-hour course is for large homes and centers, not this first license.
  • Level 2 screening for the operator, substitutes, and household members 18 and older. Ages 12–17: delinquency screen, no fingerprints. FS 402.313(3).
  • A written emergency substitute plan, current immunization records, an annual health-and-safety self-check given to parents, and 10 hours / 1 CEU every year.
  • Your homeowner’s policy does not cover day-care claims unless a rider or commercial liability policy says so. FS 627.70161. The insurer cannot cancel the house policy just because you are licensed, but it can if you are unlicensed, over capacity, or have no liability cover.
  • CACFP meal reimbursements are optional, through a sponsor, once you are licensed. Not a license requirement.

Gray · common practice

  • The one-family arrangement is the statutory gap operators use. It is real. It is also the first thing DCF will ask about if someone complains.
  • “Just helping a friend” only stays outside the statute if it is not regular paid care for two or more unrelated families.
  • People often skip the commercial liability rider and hope the house policy holds. After a claim, it usually will not. FS 627.70161 says so in plain language.
  • Informal evening or weekend sitting, with no advertising and no regular fee, is less likely to draw a licensing visit — until there is an injury or a neighbor complaint.

Tripwires

  • A second family’s children, a regular fee, and parents who drop off.
  • Advertising “daycare,” “aftercare,” or “drop-off” at the homestead.
  • More than five children unrelated to you — that is a facility, not a family home.
  • An injury, plus no license, plus a misrepresentation to a parent. If a child is badly hurt, FS 402.319(2) can make that a second-degree felony.
  • A neighbor or HOA call to DCF. HOA covenants can still prohibit the use even when zoning cannot.
Fastest legit path for usDo not turn the homestead into multi-family paid day care unless you want a DCF license. Keep farm days parent-present. Watching one other family, plus your own daughter, stays outside the family-home definition — still add a liability endorsement. If you want real day care later: apply to DCF for a licensed family child care home, finish the 35 hours, screen the household, and buy the rider. Zoning is already on your side. Your daughter counts in the cap whenever she is home.

Santa and other characters

Florida does not license Santa. Schools, trademarks, and tax still do.

There is no state license to appear as Santa, Mrs. Claus, or a generic holiday character. The gates that actually matter are trademark, school screening, sales tax, and insurance.

Strict · required

  • A local business tax receipt where the city or county requires one (ch. 205, F.S.).
  • If you sell prints, albums, or a USB of photos-with-Santa, collect Florida sales tax (6% plus the county surtax) on the whole charge, including a sitting fee tied to that sale. Fla. Admin. Code 12A-1.041. Digital-only delivery (email or download, no physical medium) is generally not taxable.
  • The state does not require liability insurance to “be Santa.” Venues and schools almost always do. Carry a certificate.
  • School grounds, students present: Jessica Lunsford Act. Contractors with student contact or control of school funds need Level 2 screening, FS 1012.465. Other contractors on campus when students are present need a fingerprint check, FS 1012.467. In Duval, vendors go through Applicant Services; volunteers go through ADDitions (registry screen; Level 2 for unsupervised or chaperone roles).
  • A child-care or school contract can also trigger Level 2 under ch. 435 / 402.

Gray · common practice

  • A farm Santa, a parade, or a private party does not need a character license.
  • Trademarked names and looks — Disney, Marvel, Elsa, Spider-Man and the rest — cannot be used in ads, flyers, invoices, or “as if licensed” meet-and-greets. Operators commonly use generic wording: “a princess,” “a superhero,” “holiday character,” “inspired-by.” That lowers, but does not erase, IP risk if the costume is an obvious copy and you charge for it.
  • A one-off farm Santa with parents present is not child care.
  • Neighborhood appearances without a school contract usually skip Level 2 — until a venue or district asks for the badge.

Tripwires

  • A flyer or Facebook ad that says “Meet Elsa” or “Spider-Man photos.”
  • Walking onto a school campus without the district vendor or volunteer badge.
  • Selling prints and not collecting sales tax.
  • A character left alone with children, or a drop-off “visit with Santa.”
  • Claiming you are “licensed,” “official,” or bonded when you are not.
Fastest legit path for usFarm Santa is the clean version: generic Santa and farm helpers, parents stay, our event liability is current, the local business tax receipt is current, and we tax the prints. For school visits, get the vendor Level 2 / district badge first (Duval Applicant Services; the same idea in Nassau and St. Johns). Do not advertise official characters unless the rights holder has licensed you.

Drop-off homeschool

Home education is parent-directed. Drop-off is a different legal box.

FS 1002.41 is the parent’s program: they notify the district superintendent, keep a portfolio, and file an annual evaluation. No required hours, no teaching certificate. We do not become their home-ed provider just because they come to the farm. The question is whether parents leave and we take over supervision, for a fee, on a regular schedule — that is child care under FS 402.302(1).
▼ DO PARENTS STAY, OR DO THEY DROP OFF?
Green · the clean path

If parents stay Parents stay for Homeschool Farm Days, or a parent-volunteer of an enrolled child is on site the whole time and still in charge. The parent remains the 1002.41 provider. This is an enrichment visit, not drop-off care.

Red · regular paid drop-off

If they drop off Parents leave, you supervise, a fee is paid, and it happens on a schedule. In the home, two or more unrelated families → family child care home license (required in Nassau). More than five unrelated children, anywhere → facility license, unless a statutory exemption actually fits.

▼ IF IT IS DROP-OFF, THESE ARE THE ONLY BOXES THAT SOMETIMES FIT
Private school · FS 1002.42 / 402.3025

Register with FLDOE (annual survey, owner fingerprints). Programs for 5-year-old kindergarten and grades 1+ are not child care. Under 3 is always child care. Ages 3–4 avoid facility licensure only if the school operates and staffs the program, a majority of the school’s enrollment is 5 or older, and staff are screened.

Church · FS 402.316

Only if the program is an integral part of a church or parochial school with regularly scheduled classes, and is accredited by — or a member of — an organization that publishes and requires health, safety, and sanitation standards. Level 2 screening still required. The statutory disclosure must appear on the website and in materials.

Summer camp · 402.302(2)

Summer day camps and vacation Bible schools are excluded from the child-care-facility definition. That is a summer fact, not a year-round loophole. Do not stretch “camp” into the school year.

Other boxes that look tempting

Membership organizations (FS 402.301(6)) are the Scout / YMCA shape: national, nonprofit, nominal annual fee, and they “do not provide child care.” Not a for-profit farm. Drop-in care: parent must remain, max 4 hours, and it still has to meet facility rules unless separately exempted.

There is no general “under X hours” cap that turns regular paid drop-off into non-care. The four-hour figures apply to drop-in care (parent must stay) and to indoor recreational facilities (which are still licensed). A signed paper saying “the parent remains the educational provider” only helps if the facts match — the parent is actually responsible, not dropping children for the day.

Fastest legit path for usKeep parents on site. That is already how Homeschool Farm Days work, and it is the only path that does not require a new license, a private-school filing, or a church-school structure.

Drop-off homeschool

What hybrids actually do, and what gets them in trouble.

Strict · required

  • A Florida home education program belongs to the parent, not to a farm, a co-op, or a teacher they hire. FS 1002.41.
  • Paid, regular drop-off is child care unless a listed exemption in ch. 402 actually applies. The label — “enrichment,” “co-op,” “hybrid,” “farm school” — does not decide it. The facts do.
  • Opening a private school means the FLDOE survey, owner fingerprints, and a real educational program. It does not legalize infant or toddler drop-off. FS 1002.42 and 402.3025.
  • A church exemption is lost if screening is skipped, and the program must print the statutory “not licensed by DCF” disclosure. FS 402.316(6).
  • Personnel who care for children 15 hours or more a week in a school, membership organization, or 402.316-exempt program are still in the screening net. FS 402.302(3).

Gray · common practice

  • The usual Northeast Florida hybrid keeps parents on site, or rotates parent-volunteers so a parent is always present. That is the model that holds up.
  • Families sign that the parent remains the 1002.41 provider and the farm is a class or field trip. Useful only when parents are not actually leaving.
  • A “church umbrella” is commonly claimed. It only works if the program truly is an integral church or parochial school under 402.316 — not a rented name.
  • “We stay under X hours, so it isn’t day care” is mostly folklore. There is no general hour cap for that.
  • Summer “camp” branding in June and July is widely used. Using it in October is a tripwire.

Tripwires

  • Advertising “drop-off homeschool,” “day camp” during the school year, or “aftercare.”
  • A regular weekly drop-off for a fee, even if you call it enrichment.
  • Children under 3 in a “school” program.
  • Claiming a church exemption you do not qualify for, or skipping the required disclosure.
  • An injury, a complaint to DCF, or taking School Readiness or scholarship money for a program that is not licensed for care.
Fastest legit path for usKeep Homeschool Farm Days parent-present. That is already the clean model, and it matches how Florida treats home education. If you later want true drop-off for school-age children, do it the long way: a licensed program, or a real private school under FS 1002.42 aimed at kindergarten and up, with screening — and still license any care for children under 3. Do not start drop-off preschool at the homestead without a DCF license.

What this means at the homestead
The bottom line

Three decisions, already in reach.

This is the short version to operate from. The statutes and agency links are on the next slide.

In-home day care

  1. Do not open a multi-family day care at the homestead unless you are ready to get a DCF family child care home license. Nassau requires a license, not a registration.
  2. One other family is the statutory line; your daughter counts in the cap if you do license. Add a liability endorsement either way — the house policy will not cover this.
  3. Keep farm days parent-present. That is an activity, not day care, and it is the fastest clean path.

Santa and characters

  1. Farm Santa is fine with no character license: generic Santa, parents stay, event liability and the business tax receipt current, tax the prints.
  2. Never advertise Elsa, Spider-Man, Disney, or Marvel unless you are licensed by the rights holder. Say “a princess” or “a superhero.”
  3. School visits need the badge first — Level 2 / Jessica Lunsford for contractors on campus when students are present. In Duval, that is Applicant Services or ADDitions.

Drop-off homeschool

  1. Parents stay. They remain the FS 1002.41 provider; we host the farm day. This is the model to keep.
  2. Regular paid drop-off is child care, whatever we name it. There is no general “under X hours” escape hatch.
  3. If drop-off is the goal later, build a licensed program or a real K+ private school. Do not start drop-off preschool without a DCF license.
Hold this while you decideFarm days and farm Santa, with parents present, are already the clean versions of all three. License only if you want true drop-off or multi-family paid care.
