1 · Franchise 101

A franchise is renting out your system, not your farm.

You're right that franchises vary as much as businesses do. They all come down to three questions: who owns the unit, who runs it, and how you get paid.

How franchisors make money (5 lines)
Initial feeOne-time, at signing. Pays for training and opening support.
RoyaltyOngoing % of gross sales (often with a monthly minimum) or a flat yearly fee.
Brand / tech fundExtra % or a flat monthly fee for marketing and software (your HubSpot + AI-search engine).
Supply marginSelling or leasing required items. For you: animals, STEM kits, trailers.
Development / transferFees for multi-unit rights, resales, and renewals.

The legal steps (do them in this order)

1. FTC testIt's legally a franchise if all 3 are true: (a) your brand, (b) significant control or help, and (c) they pay you $735+ in the first 6 months. If so, the FTC Franchise Rule applies.
2. FDDA 23-item Franchise Disclosure Document goes to the buyer at least 14 days before they sign or pay. Item 19 (earnings claims) is optional, and it's where your numbers sell.
3. FloridaFlorida doesn't make you register an FDD. It requires a one-page Franchise Exemption filing with FDACS, at $100 a year, before you offer. Fla. Stat. 817.416 bans misstating income or required investment.
4. Equity ≠ franchiseSelling 49% of a company is a securities sale, not a franchise sale. It needs an exemption (usually SEC Reg D) and an operating agreement written by counsel.

Sources: 16 CFR 436.8 (ecfr.gov); fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Sellers-of-Business-Franchises; Fla. Stat. 559.802 and 817.416 (flsenate.gov, 2026). Retrieved Nov 21, 2026.

Where Franchise #1 / Zero happens

  • Pays rent, leases, and royalty
Money flow: OpCo #1 → TechCo royalty + tech fee · OpCo #1 → PropCo land rent · OpCo #1 → AnimalCo animal/equipment lease. Future units plug into the same three pipes.
Why this is the moatTechCo's revenue is recurring royalties, tech fees, and kit sales from many units, with no barns or bites on its books. That's what earns a franchisor or tech multiple instead of a small-business multiple. The farm's ups and downs stay in OpCo, the land stays yours, and you can sell or raise on TechCo without touching your home.
The one-line pitch

Not an erratic small petting zoo. A working MVP of a vertically integrated system: the farm makes the content, the content ranks #1 in AI search, search fills the calendar, and the calendar feeds kits, memberships, and the next unit.

How a call option / buyout works

A call option is your right, never your duty, to buy their stake back at a price formula fixed today. You choose when.

Cash-flow multipleBusinesses under $1M sold at an average 2.30× cash flow in Q3 2026 (BizBuySell). That's the bean-counter floor.
Return capRevenue-share deals usually cap total payback at 1.5–2.0× the money. Newer, more volatile businesses pay 2.0–3.0× (issuer.com).
Payment rateUsually 2–5% of gross monthly revenue until the cap is hit.

4 · Package it like a car

Lease the turnkey operation. Don't sell it.

Customer lists aren't under contract, so the safest thing to sell is use, not ownership. The partner pays about what owning would cost, monthly, for 3 or 5 years. You keep title.

StructurePlain EnglishCar versionFit
Operating / FMV leasePay for use. Hand it back at the end, or buy it at fair market value then.A normal 36-month car leaseStrong
Finance (capital) leasePayments add up to the full price, then a $1 buyout. It's really an installment sale.Lease-to-ownNo. You lose the asset
Master leaseOne lease covers everything: land, barns, animals, equipment, and the brand license. Common for turnkey hotels and venues.The whole dealership on one contractBest
Sale-leasebackAn investor buys an asset, then leases it back to the operator. Cash now, and the investor holds the asset.Sell your car, rent it backSide deal for trailers or animals only. Never the land
RecommendationUse one master operating lease. PropCo (land + barns), AnimalCo (animals + equipment), and TechCo (brand + list + dashboards license) roll into one monthly payment from OpCo #1. Term: 3 years with a 2-year renewal option, priced monthly, with a discount for prepaying a year or the full term. Use a 5-year term when the partner prepays.
