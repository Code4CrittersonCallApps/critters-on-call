# Elevator pitch and 5-minute teaser

**Critters on Call · Sheehan Homestead · Speaker script · November 24, 2026 · ?v=1124c**

This is a pitch for a **tech-enabled experiences and consulting firm, with an exit.** Three cuts on one page. Use the 15-second line in a hallway. Use the ~45-second elevator when you have one breath more. Use the 5-minute teaser as a short sit-down. Use the 12–20 minute cut as the investor software demo, with [DEMO_SLIDES.html?v=1122b](assets/DEMO_SLIDES.html?v=1122b) on screen. Speak it confident and warm, in your own rhythm, plain words, no hedging.

Caveman path, both cuts: big ocean → trophy case → the bad-accountant story → not a one-trick pony → enterprise vision → exit → the ask. Sponsorship is the first ask. The SAFE is if they want in. The homestead retreat is the partnership option. Nothing here is a send.

The short cuts stay tight. The 12–20 minute cut carries the origin (homestead, Ann-Marie, the professional-programmer market) and the return. Leave the personal family-healing story off all three cuts unless you choose to tell it later, in a longer conversation. Do not name competitors. Do not open the comparison slide (11). Do not mention any entity-status issue.

## 15-second one-liner

We're building a tech-enabled experiences and consulting firm, with an exit, in a big underserved market. We started on our homestead, with my wife and a few friends. Look at the traction. I can do this quickly, anywhere.

## 45-second elevator

We're a tech-enabled experiences and consulting firm in a big, underserved market — with an exit. Ask any Jacksonville lifestyle director who they call for animals and STEM, and you'll hear a short list with us on it.

The trophy case is traction: a hundred and sixteen five-star Google reviews, almost 10,000 Facebook followers, 200,000-plus views in the last 28 days, number one in a blind Jacksonville search. About three years, no outside money — just my wife, a few friends, and PE- and VC-backed rigor.

A year ago people laughed at the consolidation idea. Perpetual-license companies lost to SaaS on unit economics. We bring the good accountant.

This holiday season, start as a sponsor. Want in? A simple SAFE, or live on the homestead as franchise partner zero.

## 12–20 minute investor teaser

This is the sit-down, software-demo style — the length of the old compliance and risk demos (those ran 11–12 minutes), with room to breathe on live screens. Mix **who / what / why / says-who**. Investor must-haves, in this order on the clock: market and opportunity, traction, team, return (how and when they get money back, the exit, how their money is protected and grown), and a clear ask. Stay credible, not autobiographical. You built this, including an app, and the proof is on the screen. Same deck rules as the five-minute: no bookings tools, no dunk lists, no dialer, no slide 11, no lingering on live Growth.

### Beat A · Who — origin and team — 0:00–2:00

**Show:** Slide 6 (the reveal), then slide 7 (Ann-Marie), then slide 9 (complementary founders).

**Say:**

A few years ago we didn't know exactly what business to start. We lived on a homestead, and Ann-Marie is a teacher, so we started STEM on Call and Critters on Call to serve families.

We're not from here, and we didn't know where the families lived, so we served communities. And we accidentally found an underserved professional market: professional programmers — lifestyle, amenity, and activity managers — with budgets, sometimes in six figures, many of them public CDD budgets.

The team is complementary on purpose. Ann-Marie is an educator, a co-founder, and she wrote the STEM curriculum. The farm was our idea. I came up in software and in PE- and VC-backed sales. I built a lead-gen department to about 120 meetings a month, and I sold to Fortune 37 through Fortune 500. I also built the systems and the app you'll see in a few minutes. That's enough about us. Let me show you the market, then the proof.

### Beat B · What and why — market and opportunity — 2:00–5:00

**Show:** Slide 2 (the market), then slide 12 (the ecosystem).

**Say:**

Jacksonville is 1.8 million people, the largest city by land area in the contiguous U.S., up 11% since 2020. Florida is number two in people added. A $129 billion GDP — fourth in Florida, 39th in the country. That's a slice of a bigger, underserved market. The research is the kind you can check: ask any lifestyle director who they call, and you'll hear a short list.

On the professional-programmer side, here's what is locked in the market math. In the four-county Jax area there are 98 CDDs and stewardship districts; we count 69 of those as having amenities. Florida has 1,046 CDDs. Public FY2026 special-events lines in a seven-budget Jax sample run a median of $40,000 and a mean of $109,000 — Julington Creek Plantation at $112,000, Tolomato / Nocatee at $475,000. So yes, some of these budgets are six figures. The named Jax working list is 7,616 organizations, 5,000 of them contactable.

My working estimate is about 4,000 organizations that do professional programming, and that current offerings can penetrate about 5% — about 10% if we won every budget we should. That 4,000-org count and those penetration rates are my estimates; they are not a locked census in the market-math files. Complementary services raise share of wallet and stickiness. We become the one-stop shop.

Here's the roll-up thesis. PE buys HVAC because there's a licensing barrier, and because the industry is regional family businesses that can be stitched together. This market is underserved because there is no barrier to entry yet — side hustlers, volunteers, a few regional vendors. So we can create the certifications and the standards. We're already Step Up certified. That's how you turn a soft category into a platform.

There's an alternative if you prefer a loan: the same stages as a 3% loan, repaid from license fees and On Call Brands revenue. Anything unpaid at month 24 converts at the $750,000 cap with a 1.25-times stock multiplier.

The operating clock is 0 to 18 months to prove the model, 18 months to 5 years to scale, and at year 5 we renew, relocate, expand, or restructure. You are buying into the company that scales, not the property that makes the first location possible.
