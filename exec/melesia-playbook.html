The sequence

Four emails and three calls over six weeks, no more than one touch a week (an email and a call in the same week is fine). Emails are short plain blocks, and each one replies on the same thread.

Why it works. Most buyers already have calendars and budgets. Maybe the budget is small and maybe the dates aren't set, but they want this. Order taking is most of the job: be easy to say yes to. For low budgets we have free, donated and partial donation options, so nobody needs to be turned away. Once a week is plenty; more than that feels pushy in this line of work.
Who we are, in one breath. I represent a small family farm that runs Critters on Call and STEM on Call: petting zoos and animal therapy for senior centers and PPECs, homeschool and farm visits for families, and goat yoga for corporate.
Churches and faith schools: drop the yoga. Say: I represent a small family farm that runs Critters on Call and STEM on Call: petting zoos, farm visits and hands on science. We work with many faith based groups and know budgets are tight, so we start with up to a 60% partial donation.

Buyer questions about W-9, insurance, payment or contracts: see What to tell buyers. When they say yes, use the scheduler.

The six weeks
Week 1Email 1 (the opener) and Call 1
Week 2Email 2, reply on the same thread, attach the one page sheet
Week 3Call 2
Week 4Email 3, reply on the same thread with proof. Optional site visit with the 3D printed goats and swag
Week 5Call 3
Week 6Email 4, the polite breakup with the giving pitch. Then stop.
When they say yes: book it
  1. Pick the date, start time and length
  2. Address, and indoor or outdoor (we need shade or a tent spot)
  3. About how many people
  4. Which service, priced from the calculator or price sheet
  5. Contact name, cell and email for the invoice
  6. Text Michael the details. He confirms the date and sends the invoice. You never take payment.

Use the pricing calculator. Custom work, STEM, assemblies, after school and anything not on the sheet goes to Michael.
